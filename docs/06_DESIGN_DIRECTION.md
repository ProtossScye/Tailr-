# 06 — Design Direction: Distinctive, Not Generic

## Feel
Confident, editorial sports-tech—not a generic AI dashboard, casino landing page, or clone of Playbook/Pikkit.

## Principles
Mobile-first; strong typography and intentional spacing; restrained base palette with one memorable accent; show losses as clearly as wins; label data “User-reported”, “Not verified”, or “Needs review”; no fake charts or metrics; thoughtful loading/error/empty states; no urgency tricks or “sure win” language.

## Voice
Direct, calm, conversational, specific, and respectful. Explain what Tailr changed and why. Never shame users for losses.

## Sample copy
- “No bets recorded yet. Add a bet you’ve placed to start your history.”
- “I’m not confident about two selections. Check them before continuing.”
- “You usually choose handicaps. Want me to prioritize them for this slip, or use a different mix?”
- “These are suggested selections, not a placed bet. Check markets and odds with your bookmaker.”
- “Based on bets you recorded. Tailr hasn’t verified these with your bookmaker.”
- “Changing a slip can change its risk. Lower combined odds do not guarantee a win.”

Before implementation, founders choose three brand adjectives, a primary user segment, 6–10 visual references, and phrases Tailr should never use. AI tools implement an agreed direction rather than inventing the brand.
